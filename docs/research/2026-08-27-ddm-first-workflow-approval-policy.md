# DDM-First Workflow and Approval-Policy Reference

Date: 2026-08-27  
Questions: Q98-Q102 plus the related self-approval, quorum, actor-assignment, and separation-of-duties semantics.  
Source policy: Use first-party public DDM evidence first. Use official Aras documentation only where DDM is silent or ambiguous. Competitor behavior is evidence, not IDEA design authority, and no UI, asset, schema, or transaction model is copied.

## Decision summary for IDEA

| Question | DDM disposition | Aras fallback for the gap | Recommended IDEA answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q98 - configuration surface | **DDM EVIDENCED.** DDM describes an inbuilt graphical workflow tool, says customers can build and deploy workflows without programming, and says its generic built-in workflows can be modified for individual customer requirements. | Not needed. | **B** - include a graphical workflow designer in the MVP. Reproduce the capability, not DDM's UI. A validated property form may accompany the canvas. |
| Q99 - definition changes and running work | **DDM AMBIGUOUS.** DDM's official built-in-workflows PDF visibly labels a workflow `Version: 1`, but the public sources do not define version identity, activation, or what a change does to an already-running workflow. | Aras creates a Workflow Process from the selected/default Workflow Map. Its current dynamic-assignment documentation says rules execute once during workflow creation and later team/item/structure changes do not trigger reassignment. | **A** - activate a new immutable policy version for new Workflow Instances; running instances remain pinned. Migration is a separate authorized action with impact preview and audit. This is an IDEA safety rule, not a claim about DDM internals. |
| Q100 - seeded approval policy | **DDM UNKNOWN.** Public DDM material proves formal decisions and configurable decision makers, but does not state a universal self-approval or author/approver/releaser separation default. | Aras assigns activities to configurable identities/roles; an Express ECO creator may or may not also hold another role, while its CCB review requires all CCB members to vote the same decision. Aras therefore does not document one universal separation rule across all workflows. | **A** - ship a safe seed: author/editor cannot approve or release that Revision; one independent approver is required; that approver may also release. Administrators can activate another validated policy version. The separation default is an IDEA governance choice. |
| Q101 - no eligible actor | **DDM UNKNOWN.** DDM documents receiving-role members and decision-maker selection, but not the empty-role result. | Aras warns administrators to populate every required workflow role before a process starts; current workflow-creation documentation says activation completes with assignments established or fails when errors occur. | **A** - block submit/start/approve/release, identify the missing role or rule, and require an administrator to repair configuration. Never auto-weaken the policy. |
| Q102 - configuration scope | **DDM AMBIGUOUS.** DDM says workflows can be modified for customer requirements, but the public source does not establish tenant isolation or mutable-policy scope. | Aras associates Workflow Maps with ItemTypes and supports configurable identities/roles; this is not evidence for multi-customer tenant isolation. | **A, superseded in scope wording by Constitution 3.3.0** - IDEA is an internal company product. Its Operating Organization owns policy versions, and no mutable policy is shared into an unauthorized environment or later separately governed internal scope. This is an IDEA governance requirement, not a commercial multi-customer tenancy claim or competitor fact. |

## DDM evidence

- DDM's [Graphical Workflow Tool](https://www.designdatamanager.com/products/graphical-workflow-tool/) says there is no limit on the different workflows customers can build, presents graphical preview and traceability, says no programming is required to build and deploy workflows, and describes the supplied workflows as generic starting points that can be modified for customer requirements. This establishes Q98 but not storage or activation semantics.
- DDM's official [Built-in Workflows PDF](https://www.designdatamanager.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/DDMs-Built-in-Workflows.pdf) shows graphical Change Order, Non-Conformance, CAPA, Internal Audit, and NPD flows. The screenshots display Workflow Name, Description, and `Version: 1`, plus actions such as Take Ownership and Return to Group. The PDF does not explain version immutability, activation, migration, or running-instance behavior.
- The DDM 2018.01 release note, [Selection of Multiple Decision Makers in DDM Workflows](https://www.designdatamanager.com/2018/02/19/ddm-2018-01-whats-new-selection-multiple-decision-makers-ddm-workflows/), documents selection of multiple decision makers for a decision step configured as `Choose on send` with `Individual selection` enabled. This proves configurable actor selection, not quorum semantics.
- The DDM 2018.09 release note, [Workflow Configuration Enhancements](https://www.designdatamanager.com/2018/10/08/ddm-2018-09-whats-new-workflow-configuration-enhancements/), documents compulsory comments on specified steps, choosing which possibly multiple receiving-role members are notified, administrative workflow start for new folders, and automatic workflow entry. It does not define self-approval, vote thresholds, separation of duties, or the empty-role outcome.
- DDM's [Formal Approvals tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v8hzVUZjm2w) and [Web workflow tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1zIyOZVxa3c) establish the visible approval/reject/resubmit/history pattern already admitted by the repository baseline. They do not establish internal transactionality or universal policy defaults.

## Aras fallback facts for DDM gaps

- Aras documents Workflow Maps as configurable process definitions with activities, paths, assignments, tasks, notifications, and voting behavior. A Workflow Activity is delivered to the assignees configured for that activity; vote choices come from the Workflow Process definition. ([Completing a Task, Platform 33](https://docs.aras.com/aras-innovator-platform-33/completing-a-task/0000019f-1799-dcff-a7bf-5f994ff90000), [Aras Innovator 31 Configurable InBasket](https://www.aras.com/community/DocumentationLibrary/ALL%20PDFs/Flare%20PDF/Flare%20PDF/Innovator%2031/Aras%20Innovator%2031%20-%20Configurable%20InBasket.pdf))
- Aras' current Unified Change Management documentation says a Workflow Process is created from the default Workflow Map and assignments are established during workflow creation. Dynamic assignment rules run once; later changes to teams, items, or structures do not trigger reassignment. ([Execution Flow](https://docs.aras.com/unified-change-management-30/execution-flow-ucm30), [Dynamic Workflow Assignments](https://docs.aras.com/dynamic-workflow-assignments-ucm30))
- Aras requires administrators to populate required change-management roles before a process starts. User identities can be grouped, and workflow roles can be backed by user or group identities. ([Understanding Change Management Roles](https://www.aras.com/community/documentationlibrary/Innovator/25/Content/Innovator%2024%20Docs/Aras%20PE%2014%20-%20Administrator%20Guide/Understanding%20Change%20Management.htm), [Users and Identities](https://www.aras.com/community/documentationlibrary/Innovator/33/Content/Innovator%2024%20Docs/User%20Visibility%20Policy%20Administration%20Guide/Users%20and%20Identities%20in%20Aras.htm))
- In Aras Express Change Management, the creator may or may not be the same person as another process role; the Change Control Board performs final review and all its members must vote the same decision. This proves configurable role overlap and an all-member decision pattern in that workflow, not a platform-wide default. ([Express Change Management, Release 35](https://www.aras.com/community/documentationlibrary/Innovator/35/Content/Innovator%2024%20Docs/Aras%20PE%2014%20-%20User's%20Guide/Express%20Change%20Management.htm))

## Related approval-policy conclusion

IDEA should make stages, exit paths, eligible roles/identities, assignment mode, quorum, required comments/evidence, self-approval, and author/approver/releaser separation explicit fields of a versioned policy. DDM directly supports the need for graphical configuration, roles, multiple decision makers, and per-step controls. Aras fills the public DDM gaps by demonstrating configurable identities, process-instantiated assignments, required-role validation, and multi-member approval. Neither source justifies hard-coded role names, silent actor fallback, or retroactive mutation of running decisions.

## Evidence limits

- DDM evidence is vendor-public and historical where dated; it does not identify a target DDM build or enabled modules.
- A visible `Version` field is not proof of immutable version records, activation rules, or migration behavior.
- DDM's multiple-decision-maker feature does not prove `any`, `all`, percentage, weighted, sequential, or concurrent quorum semantics.
- Aras is configurable, and cited Express ECO behavior is an application workflow, not a universal platform policy.
- IDEA's organization isolation, immutable policy activation, migration preview, fail-closed behavior, and seeded separation default require their own requirements and verification evidence.
